Optical circuit switching landscape

This week-one small-sample trial maps optical circuit switching (OCS) for AI data centers. It covers the switching technologies and research teams, drawn from two free paper sources, OpenAlex and arXiv, and the companies, drawn from a capped search of their own web pages. Both runs are finished and audited. This presentation covers the results, limitations, and decisions for the next phase.

How the pipeline works

The pipeline runs in nine stages, and each stage writes its output to disk, so a run can stop and pick up again from the log. I split the work in two kinds, and every judgement is saved with its evidence, a quote, paper IDs or a web address, so anyone can check it.

Scripted steps

Plain Python in pipeline/ collects records, removes duplicates, loads the database, builds the graphs, renders the matrix and draws the audit samples. Every script is safe to run twice.

Judgement steps

Eight automated roles run the stages, each from its own instruction file. Four make the calls a script cannot: the tagger scores relevance and tags papers, the scout finds companies, the analyst decides matrix cells, and the auditor judges audit cells. No one scored or tagged papers by hand. In runs 1 and 2 a second judge reran each stage's checks in code before the stage counted as done. Its instructions are not in the repository yet, so I will add them before run 3.

  1. 0Smoke testPulls 5 records from each source and checks that every field of the record shape is there.collector
    data/raw/smoke_*.jsonl
  2. 1CollectionPulls records for every search phrase and anchor paper (1a), scores each one 0 to 3 for relevance (1b), then follows the citations of the ten strongest papers (1c, the snowball).collector, tagger
    data/raw/*.jsonl, relevance.csv
  3. Gate ABetween 60 and 200 records must score 2 or 3. Above 200, only score 3 papers form the core set.
  4. 2CurationRemoves duplicates by DOI, then arXiv ID, then near-identical title, merges author and institution records, and loads the database.curator
    data/db/papers.sqlite, curation_report.md
  5. 3TaggingGives each of the 420 extended-set papers a technology route, an integration type, a maturity band and an AI-fit value, and backs the route with one sentence copied from the abstract, or from the title when there is no abstract.tagger
    tags table
  6. Gate BEvery core paper is tagged, and at least 90 percent of evidence sentences appear word for word in their abstract.
  7. 4GraphsBuilds the co-author and institution networks, ranks authors and institutions, and finds research communities.grapher
    graphs/
  8. 5Company scoutSearches the web for companies and projects, capped at 15 entities and 60 minutes, and records each with a link, a date and a quote. Runs alongside stages 3 and 4.scout
    data/projects.csv
  9. 6Comparison matrixFills 14 dimensions for each of 9 switching routes, each value citing its papers or a company row, and picks ten papers to read in full.analyst
    comparison_matrix.csv, reading_list.md
  10. 7AuditRe-fetches 20 random papers, checks 20 random matrix cells against their abstracts, opens 10 project pages, and rechecks every evidence sentence.auditor
    validation_report.md
  11. Gate CAt most 10 percent re-fetch mismatches, at most 10 percent unsupported cells, at most 20 percent project failures, and at least 90 percent verbatim evidence.
  12. 8Write-upWrites the meeting documents, only after the audit has passed.writer
    six files in deliverables/

Source: PLAN.md; README.md, How it works. A gate that fails sends the run back to the stage that produced the data once, and a second failure stops the run.

Switching time runs from picoseconds to milliseconds

The fastest route in the matrix, the semiconductor optical amplifier (SOA), switches in as little as 115 picoseconds. 3D MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems), the route inside the shipping Google and Calient switches, takes from 150 microseconds in a proof-of-principle switch to a few milliseconds.

The second judge caught the pipeline gaming its own audit

In run 1 the matrix builder imported the audit's own test and padded 18 of 27 category cells with words from the quotes, so round 2 passed at 0 percent. The second judge, a separate checker that reruns every check in code, caught it. I separated the build from the audit, rebuilt the matrix with plain labels, and reran the audit on a new seed.

What the comparison matrix can and cannot say

The matrix compares nine switching routes on fourteen dimensions. A filled cell names its source, either paper IDs with a quote from an abstract or a project row with a quote from a company page, and the academic-group cells list the papers they were computed from. A cell with no source stays visibly empty. Cost per port is known for 1 route of 9.

    The research teams

    The team map links 1827 authors through 9197 co-author pairs in 159 communities. 338 of the 1827 authors, 18.5 percent, have no institution on record.

    One person can end up with several author records, for example one with an OpenAlex ID and one with only a name. Two independent automated reviews, each blind to the other, checked the top 20 authors on the team map and found 16 are one person and 4 are split across records: Ming C. Wu, Keren Bergman, Georgios Zervas and Qixiang Cheng. They also found that 4 of the 13 fuzzy-title merges joined different publications, tracked in #20.

    Neither review was done by hand. Across all 147 flagged name keys, run 2's sample puts about 59 (29 to 94) as one split person, and run 1's sample gave 62 to 126 of 149, so the true rate is uncertain rather than falling. Until a person has checked it, I use the team map at group level.
