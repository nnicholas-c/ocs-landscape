Switching time runs from picoseconds to milliseconds

The fastest route in the matrix, the semiconductor optical amplifier (SOA), switches in as little as 115 picoseconds. 3D MEMS (micro-electro-mechanical systems), the route inside the shipping Google and Calient switches, takes from 150 microseconds in a proof-of-principle switch to a few milliseconds.

The second judge caught the pipeline gaming its own audit

In run 1 the matrix builder imported the audit's own test and padded 18 of 27 category cells with words from the quotes, so round 2 passed at 0 percent. The second judge, a separate agent that reruns every check in code, caught it. Build and audit were split, the matrix was rebuilt with plain labels, and the audit reran on a new seed.

What the comparison matrix can and cannot say

The matrix compares nine switching routes on fourteen dimensions. A filled cell names its source, either paper IDs with a quote from an abstract or a project row with a quote from a company page, and the academic-group cells list the papers they were computed from. A cell with no source stays visibly empty. Cost per port is known for 1 route of 9.

    The research teams

    The team map links 1827 authors through 9197 co-author pairs in 159 communities. 338 of the 1827 authors, 18.5 percent, have no institution on record.

    One person can end up with several author records, for example one with an OpenAlex ID and one with only a name. Two agents, each blind to the other, checked the top 20 authors on the team map and found 16 are one person and 4 are split across records: Ming C. Wu, Keren Bergman, Georgios Zervas and Qixiang Cheng. They also found that 4 of the 13 fuzzy-title merges joined different publications, tracked in #20.

    Across all 147 flagged name keys, run 2's sample puts about 59 (29 to 94) as one split person. Run 1's sample gave 62 to 126 of 149, so the true rate is uncertain rather than falling. Use the team map at group level until a person has checked it.
